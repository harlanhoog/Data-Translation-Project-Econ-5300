# Causal Inference: The Effect of Harsher DUI Punishments on Recidivism

## 📌 Project Overview
**Objective:** To determine whether the legal penalties imposed on drunk drivers actually deter repeat offenses: 32]. This project implements a sharp Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD) to evaluate the causal impact of crossing the 0.08 Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) legal threshold on future recidivism rates in Washington State: 37, 155].

## 📊 Data Description
The analysis utilizes administrative records from 214,558 DUI stops: 38]. 
* **Target Variable:** Recidivism (binary indicator of whether a driver repeats a drunk-driving offense after the initial stop): 45].
* **Running Variable:** The minimum value between two administered BAC tests, centering the legal cutoff at 0.08: 56, 65].
* **Covariates:** Age, gender, race, and accident involvement at the scene: 82].

## 🛠️ Methodology & Tools
This project was conducted using **R** (leveraging packages such as `fixest`, `rdrobust`, and `tidyverse`) and documented via **Quarto**: 38]. The statistical approach focused on isolating causal effects:
* **Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD):** Deployed a sharp RDD, using a local linear regression approach to compare drivers just below and just above the 0.08 legal limit: 98, 155].
* **Assumption Validation:** Conducted density checks to confirm the absence of running variable manipulation (no bunching at the cutoff) and verified that all observable covariates evolved smoothly across the threshold: 79, 90].
* **Bandwidth Optimization:** Selected a narrow primary bandwidth of ±0.02 (0.06 to 0.10 BAC) to strictly satisfy the RDD requirement of local comparability, retaining over 40,000 observations: 117, 118].
* **Robustness Checks:** Evaluated the model against alternative bandwidths (±0.03 and ±0.05) to ensure the stability of the estimated treatment effect: 140]. 
* **Standard Errors:** Applied heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors to account for the binary nature of the recidivism outcome variable: 121].

## 💡 Key Findings
* **Significant Deterrent Effect:** Crossing the 0.08 BAC threshold, which triggers harsher legal sanctions, is predicted to reduce the probability of future recidivism by approximately 1.47 percentage points: 123, 164].
* **Meaningful Impact:** While 1.47 percentage points may appear modest, it represents a substantial reduction relative to the baseline recidivism probability of 10.74% for drivers sitting just below the legal cutoff: 165].
* **Robust Results:** Robustness checks confirmed that the deterrent effect remains stable and statistically significant across different bandwidths, consistently showing reductions ranging from 1.47 to 1.87 percentage points: 145, 146].
